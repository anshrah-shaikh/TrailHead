About Trailhead

Career advice, minus the lecture.

Most career guidance either comes as a 40-slide PDF nobody opens, or a 15-minute chat that's over before you've decided anything. Trailhead is neither — it's a reference you can actually browse, at 11pm, on your own time.

Why this exists

By the time you're in class 9, people start asking what you want to "be." Most of us don't have a clean answer, and the honest advice we get is often just "keep your options open" — which is true, but not exactly useful on its own.

Trailhead exists to make "keeping your options open" a little more concrete: a plain list of what a career actually involves, which stream it usually needs, and the specific steps between where you are now and doing that job for real.

How the pathways work

Each entry is built the same way: a short, plain-language description of what the role actually is day-to-day, followed by the qualification steps in order — starting from your class 10 or 12 stream and ending at the point you'd be job-ready.

Some pathways run through a standard degree. Others go through India's National Skills Qualification Framework (NSQF), a vocational route that lets you train directly for a trade without a long academic detour. Both are shown the same way, side by side, because neither is a lesser option.

What's in the guide

500 careers, from acupuncturist to zoologist
4 streams — Science, Commerce, Arts and Vocational
1–6 clear steps per pathway, no filler

How to actually use this site

  1. Start broad. If you don't know your stream yet, take the quiz — it's six questions, not a personality test.
  2. Skim, don't study. Open the careers page and click into anything that catches your eye, even careers that sound unlikely for you.
  3. Compare pathways, not just titles. Two careers can sound similar and need completely different routes — read the steps, not just the name.
  4. Talk to someone after, not instead. This is a starting point for a conversation with a teacher, parent or counsellor, not a replacement for one.
Community Engagement Project · Semester 5 · TYBSc CS

Built for the community, not just the classroom.

Trailhead was created as part of the Community Engagement Project for Semester 5 of the TYBSc Computer Science programme. The idea is simple: make career exploration a little easier to access, especially for students who are still figuring out what they enjoy, what they are good at, and what options exist beyond the careers they already know.

As part of the project, our team also takes the career-guidance initiative beyond the website through sessions and interactions with students at other colleges and educational spaces. The website acts as a resource they can return to after those conversations.

A note on the data

The pathway details on this site are adapted from published career-guidance material for Indian students and cleaned up for readability. Course names, entrance exams and durations change fairly often, so treat every pathway here as a strong starting map, not the final word — always confirm current requirements with the institution or board directly before making a decision.
